Model, assumptions, and verification

Equations: dS/dt = −βSI/N; dI/dt = βSI/N − γI; dR/dt = γI. Euler integration with Δt = 0.1 day, values sampled at each integer day. β and γ have units day⁻¹; S, I, R are people. Simulated values are continuous and rounded only for display.

Assumptions: closed population of 1,000; homogeneous mixing; no births, deaths, migration, reinfection, incubation period, stochasticity, or changing behavior. This is an educational simulation, not a real-world epidemic forecast.

Numerical example: At day zero with β = 0.35, γ = 0.10, S = 990, I = 10, N = 1,000, the new-infection rate is 0.35 × 990 × 10 / 1,000 = 3.465 people/day; recovery rate is 0.10 × 10 = 1 person/day; therefore dI/dt = 2.465 people/day.

Boundary cases: β = 0 causes no new infections, so I cannot increase; I(0) = 0 causes no outbreak. Both are available through the controls.

Scientific background: Kermack & McKendrick (1927), “A Contribution to the Mathematical Theory of Epidemics,” DOI:10.1098/rspa.1927.0118. For conceptual background see CDC. The model and figures are implemented locally in JavaScript without external data or libraries.

Interaction guide: Change β, γ, or initially infected to restart and recompute; drag the day slider, play/pause, or step; hover the epidemic curves to inspect actual values; choose a comparison scenario. Press Reset to restore defaults.
